## Product-truth safeguards

Every platform requires images that accurately represent the sold SKU. Iwantphoto should therefore preserve the uploaded product’s shape, proportions, colour, material, packaging and labels; never invent features, accessories, certification, performance claims, prices, discounts, guarantee language, watermarks, retailer branding or unavailable variants. For output types requiring dimensions, variants, packaging contents or real copy, the user should supply or confirm the exact details before publication. Generated images are production aids, not an automatic compliance guarantee.

## Feature scope adopted in Iwantphoto

The first product release generates a compact, channel-aware suite from one to three product uploads: a catalog-safe main image, an alternate presentation, a material/detail image, and a lifestyle image. The first upload is the primary asset that is retained in the customer library; the other angles are transmitted only as temporary AI references to reconcile product details and are not independently saved or metered. The workflow first asks a vision model to draft an editable traditional-Chinese product brief from all uploaded views. It separates visible observations from **questions the merchant must verify**. The user sees drafting progress and a realistic 20–30 second guidance message; a failed retry never clears the currently editable brief. The current browser session retains the five most recent AI and pre-retry versions, supports field-by-field comparison, lets the merchant name a version, and lets the merchant restore a selected version before generation. Before generation, the merchant must review and confirm a product name, description, factual details, approved supplementary-image callouts, and acceptable use contexts. An optional second AI step then produces an editable platform-aware title and exactly five listing bullets **only from that confirmed record**, with uncertainty preserved as review notes rather than copy. For **Lazada Thailand** and **Shopee Thailand**, it produces Thai listing copy, gives live title/description counters, and exposes editable category, brand, variation, category-attribute and package-logistics fields. The Lazada Thailand export also includes box contents; both Thailand exports remain starting templates rather than category-approved uploads. The merchant can copy the title or five-point description, export the confirmed brief as a UTF-8-BOM CSV or Excel workbook, and use a selected SKU to make a platform-specific Amazon, Shopee, Lazada Thailand, Shopee Thailand, Meta or Google starting template. Formula-like values are escaped, while category-specific attributes, pricing, stock and image URLs are intentionally left for the merchant’s current official platform template and final verification. Confirmed product data can be saved to a selected project under a merchant-entered SKU; records are account- and project-scoped, update in place for the same SKU, and a saved listing copy is reapplied only to its matching platform. Reference product photos must still be supplied when generating a new image suite. They can select only the desired image roles, so processing-credit consumption is exactly the selected output count. A merchant can create multiple named account brand presets, each with a restrained accent colour, typography direction and optional approved Logo, choose an account default, and assign a preferred preset to a photo project. A project preset is used ahead of the account default, while a merchant can still choose an unsaved one-off style for one suite. An uploaded Logo is kept as a protected account file and supplied as a visual reference **only** for eligible supplementary studio, feature and lifestyle images; the catalog main image never receives a Logo, and Google Merchant Center assets never receive a Logo overlay. None of these settings authorizes the system to invent a brand name, Logo, claim or other copy. The catalog main image is always text-free and must show all visually verifiable sold items together; the system must not duplicate an item to imply an unseen pack count. A supplementary feature image may use a small number of verified textual callouts only when the merchant has supplied the exact approved wording; otherwise it remains a text-free detail image. Shopee and marketplace-specific specification/packaging cards are represented as **review prompts** rather than invented text-bearing outputs. Google Merchant Center suites are a special case: each AI output is converted to PNG and receives the required IPTC Extension `DigitalSourceType=trainedAlgorithmicMedia` XMP property before it is saved to the customer’s library or offered for download. This technical marker does not replace a merchant’s final SKU, category, image-policy, feed and diagnostic review.

### Merchant-guided draft revisions

After an initial brief has appeared, the merchant can enter a free-text instruction directly beneath the editable product fields, for example asking for a simpler product name or removal of an uncertain health statement. The request is sent together with the current working draft and the same product reference photos. A successful revision becomes a new session version for comparison and restoration; a failed revision deliberately keeps both the current fields and the requested instruction intact. The model may follow a writing or correction preference, but it must not treat the request as evidence of a dosage, health claim, ingredient, capacity, certification, safety statement, compatibility, package quantity or other unverified fact. Those requests remain review questions until the merchant supplies verifiable information.

The revision field also provides one-tap instructions for **更精簡**, **改專業語氣**, **移除醫療字眼**, **翻譯成英文**, **翻譯成簡體中文**, **翻譯成日文**, **翻譯成泰文**, and **只保留可見資料**. A shortcut is appended to, rather than replacing, the merchant’s existing request, and the customer may edit its wording before choosing to update the draft. Translation requests convert every editable brief field, including verification questions, to the requested language. These controls do not consume image-processing credits and do not authorize the model to add product claims or specifications not present in the confirmed source material.

### Reference readiness, progress and recovery

Immediately after upload, the suite performs a local readiness check using dimensions, file size, sampled edge variation and center-to-background contrast. It **does not inspect product claims or alter the image**. The check flags a low-resolution, heavily compressed, low-contrast or visually busy reference so that the merchant can replace it before drafting; it remains advisory because unusual products and legitimate white-on-white catalog images can still require human judgment. During suite creation, the dialog shows the current planned output role, an elapsed-time estimate and a conservative remaining-time estimate. This is not provider-reported completion and never reaches 100% until the server returns a result. If only certain roles fail after both image providers have been tried, completed assets remain available and the merchant can retry just the failed role. The retry reads an authenticated, account-owned saved source rather than uploading or metering the main reference again. If every role fails, the workflow reports a recovery path and does not charge incomplete outputs; in every case, the merchant should verify the final result against the real SKU before publication.

### Distinct image-role compositions

The four selected roles are deliberately not four white-background duplicates. The **catalog main** remains the only pure-white, front-facing, platform-safe image. The **studio supporting image** uses a distinct three-quarter or side-facing camera angle with a non-white studio environment. The **verified detail** is a close crop of a real visible label, cap, material, texture, construction or interface element. The **lifestyle image** uses the merchant-confirmed use context to place the same product in a subtle, non-white ownership or use setting; generic environment items are context only and must not imply a sold bundle, accessory, health result or unsupported claim.

After each supplementary image is generated, Iwantphoto samples its composition. A lifestyle, studio or detail output that is still near-white like a catalog main, or is materially too similar to an earlier suite output, is regenerated once automatically with a targeted composition correction and **without charging a second image credit**. If the corrected result still does not pass this advisory check, the result card clearly explains the concern and offers a single-image “improve composition” retry. This safety net raises the chance of a varied suite but does not replace the merchant’s product and platform review.

### Lifestyle scene style

When **使用情境圖** is selected, the merchant can choose **按產品資料自動選擇**, **家居**, **辦公室**, or **戶外** before generating the suite. The selected setting applies only to the lifestyle card; it does not weaken the catalog main-image rule or change the studio and detail role contracts. Each setting remains subordinate to the merchant-confirmed use contexts and product evidence. For example, an outdoor request will not imply weather resistance, food/medicine consumption, animal use, risky activity, or a performance claim. Generic surroundings are contextual only and must never become unconfirmed sold accessories or a claimed bundle.


### Per-image redesign and reusable workflow templates

Each completed, account-saved suite image now has its own **redesign instruction** field. A merchant can describe an art-direction change—for example, a warmer window-side composition, a different office environment, or a closer material view—and regenerate only that selected role. The request is treated strictly as direction for composition, framing, lighting, background, or non-sold context. It cannot establish a new product fact, claim, certification, package count, label translation, accessory, or SKU. The selected image uses one processing credit (monthly allowance first, then prepaid credits where applicable), while keeping the owned saved source as the reference rather than uploading a duplicate source file.

Signed-in merchants can also save a named **workflow template** containing the marketplace, selected image roles, lifestyle style, selected project, and brand presentation. Templates deliberately exclude source images, SKU, product brief, listing text, and generated assets. Applying a template restores these reusable settings only; customers still upload or select the product reference, review product facts, and confirm generation for each new SKU. If a stored project or brand preset is no longer available, Iwantphoto keeps the safe remaining settings and asks the merchant to select a replacement.
